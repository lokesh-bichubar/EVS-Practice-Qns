📌 Multidisciplinary Nature of Environmental Studies

1
What is the primary nature of Environmental Studies?
A.Multidisciplinary
B.Bidisciplinary
C.Unidisciplinary
D.None of these
✅ Answer: A. Multidisciplinary
💡 Explanation: Environmental Studies combines science, social science, economics, law and ethics, so it is multidisciplinary.
2
Public awareness about environment is necessary because:
A.It affects everyone
B.It reduces taxes
C.It is a trend
D.It is a government rule
✅ Answer: A. It affects everyone
💡 Explanation: Environmental problems affect all of society, so informed public participation is essential.

📌 Ecosystems

3
Which of the following is an abiotic component of an ecosystem?
A.Fungi
B.Water
C.Plants
D.Animals
✅ Answer: B. Water
💡 Explanation: Water is a non-living physical factor; plants, animals and fungi are living (biotic).
4
What is the flow of energy in an ecosystem?
A.Cyclic
B.Multidirectional
C.Unidirectional
D.Bidirectional
✅ Answer: C. Unidirectional
💡 Explanation: Energy moves one way from the Sun to producers to consumers and is lost as heat, unlike nutrients, which cycle.
5
Which of these represents a network of interconnected food chains?
A.Food chain
B.Ecological pyramid
C.Energy flow
D.Food web
✅ Answer: D. Food web
💡 Explanation: A food web links many food chains, showing multiple feeding relationships.
6
Organisms that synthesize their own food are called:
A.Producers
B.Consumers
C.Decomposers
D.Scavengers
✅ Answer: A. Producers
💡 Explanation: Producers (autotrophs) make food from inorganic substances using light or chemical energy.
7
Which of the following is an aquatic ecosystem?
A.Grassland
B.Estuary
C.Forest
D.Desert
✅ Answer: B. Estuary
💡 Explanation: An estuary, where a river meets the sea, is an aquatic ecosystem; the others are terrestrial.
8
What is the role of decomposers in an ecosystem?
A.Consume plants
B.Produce energy
C.Recycle nutrients
D.Hunt animals
✅ Answer: C. Recycle nutrients
💡 Explanation: Decomposers break down dead organisms and waste, returning nutrients to the soil and water.
9
Which pyramid is always upright?
A.Pyramid of Numbers
B.Pyramid of Energy
C.Pyramid of Biomass
D.None of these
✅ Answer: B. Pyramid of Energy
💡 Explanation: Energy decreases at each level because of losses as heat, so the energy pyramid is always upright. Numbers and biomass pyramids can be inverted.
10
Which ecosystem has the lowest primary productivity?
A.Estuary
B.Desert
C.Grassland
D.Forest
✅ Answer: B. Desert
💡 Explanation: Deserts have scarce water and sparse vegetation, giving very low primary productivity.
11
The study of interactions between organisms and their environment is:
A.Botany
B.Geology
C.Ecology
D.Zoology
✅ Answer: C. Ecology
💡 Explanation: Ecology studies organisms and their environment; botany covers plants, zoology animals and geology the Earth.
12
Which is a primary consumer?
A.Decomposer
B.Omnivore
C.Carnivore
D.Herbivore
✅ Answer: D. Herbivore
💡 Explanation: Herbivores eat plants, so they occupy the second trophic level as primary consumers.
13
Lakes and ponds are examples of:
A.Lotic ecosystems
B.Lentic ecosystems
C.Terrestrial ecosystems
D.Marine ecosystems
✅ Answer: B. Lentic ecosystems
💡 Explanation: Lentic ecosystems have standing water, such as lakes and ponds.
14
Rivers and streams are examples of:
A.Deserts
B.Lentic ecosystems
C.Lotic ecosystems
D.Estuaries
✅ Answer: C. Lotic ecosystems
💡 Explanation: Lotic ecosystems have flowing water, such as rivers and streams.
15
An ecosystem comprises:
A.Both living and non-living components
B.Only plants and animals
C.Only non-living components
D.Only living components
✅ Answer: A. Both living and non-living components
💡 Explanation: An ecosystem is a functional unit of biotic and abiotic components interacting together.
16
Which is not a type of ecological pyramid?
A.Pyramid of Numbers
B.Pyramid of Wealth
C.Pyramid of Biomass
D.Pyramid of Energy
✅ Answer: B. Pyramid of Wealth
💡 Explanation: Ecological pyramids represent numbers, biomass or energy. 'Wealth' is not one of them.
17
Autotrophs are also known as:
A.Consumers
B.Heterotrophs
C.Producers
D.Decomposers
✅ Answer: C. Producers
💡 Explanation: Autotrophs produce organic food from inorganic sources, so they are the producers.
18
Heterotrophs are also known as:
A.Producers
B.Plants
C.Autotrophs
D.Consumers
✅ Answer: D. Consumers
💡 Explanation: Heterotrophs cannot make their own food and must consume other organisms.
19
The ultimate source of energy for most ecosystems is:
A.The Sun
B.Soil
C.Water
D.Wind
✅ Answer: A. The Sun
💡 Explanation: Solar energy drives photosynthesis and thus nearly all ecosystems.

📌 Water Quality and Water Treatment

20
The process of water movement through the environment is called:
A.Hydrological cycle
B.Oxygen cycle
C.Carbon cycle
D.Nitrogen cycle
✅ Answer: A. Hydrological cycle
💡 Explanation: The hydrological (water) cycle covers evaporation, condensation, precipitation and runoff.
21
Which of the following is known as the universal solvent?
A.Acetone
B.Water
C.Alcohol
D.Benzene
✅ Answer: B. Water
💡 Explanation: Water's polarity lets it dissolve more substances than any other common liquid.
22
DO stands for:
A.Direct Oxygen
B.Demand Oxygen
C.Dissolved Oxygen
D.Diluted Oxygen
✅ Answer: C. Dissolved Oxygen
💡 Explanation: DO is Dissolved Oxygen, the oxygen available to aquatic life and an indicator of water quality.
23
High BOD indicates:
A.Low organic pollution
B.High dissolved oxygen
C.Pure water
D.High organic pollution
✅ Answer: D. High organic pollution
💡 Explanation: High Biochemical Oxygen Demand means lots of biodegradable organic matter and heavy oxygen consumption by microbes, i.e. polluted water.
24
COD stands for:
A.Chemical Oxygen Demand
B.Carbon Oxide Demand
C.Chemical Oxide Demand
D.Carbon Oxygen Demand
✅ Answer: A. Chemical Oxygen Demand
💡 Explanation: COD is Chemical Oxygen Demand, the oxygen needed to chemically oxidise organic and inorganic matter in water.
25
The settling of solid particles in water is called:
A.Filtration
B.Sedimentation
C.Flocculation
D.Coagulation
✅ Answer: B. Sedimentation
💡 Explanation: Sedimentation is the settling of suspended solids under gravity.
26
Which chemical is commonly used as a coagulant in water treatment?
A.Chlorine
B.Fluoride
C.Alum
D.Ozone
✅ Answer: C. Alum
💡 Explanation: Alum makes fine suspended particles stick together; chlorine and ozone disinfect, fluoride is added for dental health.
27
The clumping together of fine particles in water treatment is called:
A.Filtration
B.Sedimentation
C.pH adjustment
D.Flocculation
✅ Answer: D. Flocculation
💡 Explanation: Flocculation joins destabilised fine particles into larger flocs that settle easily.
28
What does a pH of 7 indicate?
A.Neutral
B.Acidic
C.Basic
D.Alkaline
✅ Answer: A. Neutral
💡 Explanation: pH 7 is neutral; below 7 is acidic and above 7 is basic or alkaline.
29
What percentage of Earth's water is freshwater?
A.Around 50%
B.Around 97%
C.Around 70%
D.Around 3%
✅ Answer: D. Around 3%
💡 Explanation: About 97% is saltwater, leaving roughly 3% as freshwater, much of it locked in ice and groundwater.
30
Filtration in water treatment removes:
A.Pathogens only
B.Suspended impurities
C.Dissolved gases
D.Hardness
✅ Answer: B. Suspended impurities
💡 Explanation: Filtration passes water through sand or other media that trap suspended particles.

📌 Natural Resources and Associated Problems

31
Which of the following is a renewable resource?
A.Petroleum
B.Natural gas
C.Solar energy
D.Coal
✅ Answer: C. Solar energy
💡 Explanation: Solar energy is inexhaustible on human timescales; coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels.
32
Soil erosion can be prevented by:
A.Overgrazing
B.Deforestation
C.Mining
D.Afforestation
✅ Answer: D. Afforestation
💡 Explanation: Planting trees binds soil and slows runoff; the other options accelerate erosion.
33
The conversion of fertile land into desert is called:
A.Desertification
B.Deforestation
C.Soil erosion
D.Landslide
✅ Answer: A. Desertification
💡 Explanation: Desertification is the degradation of fertile land into desert-like conditions.
34
Which activity is a major cause of deforestation?
A.Using solar panels
B.Agriculture expansion
C.Reforestation
D.Rainwater harvesting
✅ Answer: B. Agriculture expansion
💡 Explanation: Clearing forest for crops and farmland is a leading cause of deforestation.
35
Dams are built primarily for:
A.Causing earthquakes
B.Increasing floods
C.Hydroelectric power and irrigation
D.Deforestation
✅ Answer: C. Hydroelectric power and irrigation
💡 Explanation: Dams store water for power generation, irrigation, drinking supply and flood control.
36
A major environmental impact of mining is:
A.Soil fertility enhancement
B.Reduction of pollution
C.Increase in forest cover
D.Loss of biodiversity
✅ Answer: D. Loss of biodiversity
💡 Explanation: Mining removes vegetation and disturbs land, leading to biodiversity loss.
37
Resettlement and rehabilitation are major issues associated with:
A.Dam building
B.Planting trees
C.Using wind energy
D.Organic farming
✅ Answer: A. Dam building
💡 Explanation: Big dams submerge land and displace people, who need resettlement and rehabilitation.
38
Over-exploitation of groundwater leads to:
A.Floods
B.Lowering of water table
C.Increase in groundwater level
D.Increase in rainfall
✅ Answer: B. Lowering of water table
💡 Explanation: Pumping more than is recharged lowers the water table.
39
Which is a non-renewable energy source?
A.Wind
B.Tidal
C.Coal
D.Geothermal
✅ Answer: C. Coal
💡 Explanation: Coal is a fossil fuel; wind, tidal and geothermal are renewable.
40
Conflicts over water sharing between states are called:
A.International water disputes
B.Groundwater disputes
C.Surface water disputes
D.Inter-state water disputes
✅ Answer: D. Inter-state water disputes
💡 Explanation: Disputes between Indian states over rivers such as the Cauvery are inter-state water disputes.
41
World food problems are exacerbated by:
A.Rapid population growth
B.Sustainable agriculture
C.Organic farming
D.Reduced food waste
✅ Answer: A. Rapid population growth
💡 Explanation: Rapid population growth increases demand faster than supply, worsening food problems.
42
Overgrazing leads to:
A.Increased soil fertility
B.Soil erosion
C.Water conservation
D.Forest growth
✅ Answer: B. Soil erosion
💡 Explanation: Excess grazing strips vegetation and leaves soil exposed to erosion.
43
Excessive use of fertilizers causes:
A.Acid rain
B.Global warming
C.Eutrophication
D.Ozone depletion
✅ Answer: C. Eutrophication
💡 Explanation: Fertilizer runoff adds nitrogen and phosphorus to water, causing algal blooms and eutrophication.
44
Pesticides mainly cause:
A.Radioactive pollution
B.Noise pollution
C.Thermal pollution
D.Soil and water pollution
✅ Answer: D. Soil and water pollution
💡 Explanation: Pesticides contaminate soil and leach into water bodies.
45
Which is an alternate energy source?
A.Biogas
B.Petrol
C.Kerosene
D.Diesel
✅ Answer: A. Biogas
💡 Explanation: Biogas, produced from organic waste, is an alternate energy source; the others are fossil fuels.
46
EIA stands for:
A.Environmental Issue Assessment
B.Environmental Impact Assessment
C.Ecological Impact Analysis
D.Environmental Information Agency
✅ Answer: B. Environmental Impact Assessment
💡 Explanation: EIA is Environmental Impact Assessment, a study of a project's likely effects on the environment.
47
The main objective of EIA is to:
A.Promote deforestation
B.Increase pollution levels
C.Predict environmental consequences of a project
D.Halt all development
✅ Answer: C. Predict environmental consequences of a project
💡 Explanation: EIA predicts environmental impacts so they can be avoided or reduced before the project proceeds.
48
Land degradation is primarily caused by:
A.Using renewable energy
B.Reforestation
C.Wildlife conservation
D.Unscientific agricultural practices
✅ Answer: D. Unscientific agricultural practices
💡 Explanation: Poor farming practices such as over-irrigation and excess chemicals degrade land quality.
49
Landslides are most common in:
A.Hilly and mountainous regions
B.Plains
C.Coastal areas
D.Deserts
✅ Answer: A. Hilly and mountainous regions
💡 Explanation: Steep, unstable slopes in hilly regions with heavy rainfall are most prone to landslides.
50
Which is a surface water resource?
A.Aquifer
B.River
C.Groundwater
D.Well
✅ Answer: B. River
💡 Explanation: Rivers are surface water; aquifers, wells and groundwater are below ground.
51
Drought is caused by:
A.Tsunami
B.Excessive rainfall
C.Prolonged shortage of rainfall
D.Melting of glaciers
✅ Answer: C. Prolonged shortage of rainfall
💡 Explanation: Drought is a prolonged period of deficient rainfall.
52
Floods can be mitigated by:
A.Deforestation
B.Mining
C.Urbanization
D.Building dams and reservoirs
✅ Answer: D. Building dams and reservoirs
💡 Explanation: Dams and reservoirs hold back excess flow and reduce flood peaks.
53
The green revolution led to:
A.Increase in crop yield using modern techniques
B.Banning fertilizers
C.Decrease in agricultural production
D.Promoting organic farming
✅ Answer: A. Increase in crop yield using modern techniques
💡 Explanation: The Green Revolution raised yields through HYV seeds, fertilizers and irrigation.
54
Modern agriculture relies heavily on:
A.Traditional farming methods
B.Chemical fertilizers and pesticides
C.Manual labor
D.Rainfed irrigation
✅ Answer: B. Chemical fertilizers and pesticides
💡 Explanation: Modern farming depends on chemical fertilizers, pesticides and mechanisation.
55
Growing energy needs are primarily driven by:
A.Conservation efforts
B.Decrease in population
C.Industrialization and population growth
D.Reduction in technology
✅ Answer: C. Industrialization and population growth
💡 Explanation: Demand rises with industrial growth and increasing population.
56
Which energy source has the least environmental impact?
A.Coal
B.Natural gas
C.Nuclear power
D.Wind energy
✅ Answer: D. Wind energy
💡 Explanation: Wind power has low emissions and little pollution compared with the other options.
57
Biofuels are derived from:
A.Biological materials (plants/algae)
B.Wind
C.Uranium
D.Fossil fuels
✅ Answer: A. Biological materials (plants/algae)
💡 Explanation: Biofuels such as ethanol and biodiesel come from plants, algae or organic waste.
58
Which of the following is a consequence of dam construction?
A.Increase in soil fertility
B.Submergence of forest land
C.Prevention of all earthquakes
D.Decrease in waterborne diseases
✅ Answer: B. Submergence of forest land
💡 Explanation: Reservoirs submerge forests and habitats. The other options are false or exaggerated.
59
Waterlogging is often a result of:
A.Using drip irrigation
B.Planting drought-resistant crops
C.Excessive irrigation without proper drainage
D.Droughts
✅ Answer: C. Excessive irrigation without proper drainage
💡 Explanation: Over-irrigation with poor drainage raises the water table and saturates the soil.
60
Which of these is a localized environmental problem?
A.Ozone depletion
B.Global warming
C.Climate change
D.Noise pollution
✅ Answer: D. Noise pollution
💡 Explanation: Noise pollution is confined to specific areas; the rest are global issues.

📌 Biodiversity and Its Conservation

61
Biodiversity refers to:
A.Variety of life on Earth
B.Non-living components
C.Only animals in a zoo
D.Only plants in a forest
✅ Answer: A. Variety of life on Earth
💡 Explanation: Biodiversity covers the variety of all living organisms, their genes and ecosystems.
62
Which is NOT a level of biodiversity?
A.Species diversity
B.Population diversity
C.Ecosystem diversity
D.Genetic diversity
✅ Answer: B. Population diversity
💡 Explanation: Biodiversity is classified into genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
63
Genetic diversity within a species helps in:
A.Habitat destruction
B.Extinction
C.Adaptation to changing environments
D.Reducing population size
✅ Answer: C. Adaptation to changing environments
💡 Explanation: Greater genetic variation improves a species' ability to adapt and survive.
64
India has how many biogeographic zones?
A.15
B.5
C.20
D.10
✅ Answer: D. 10
💡 Explanation: India is classified into 10 biogeographic zones.
65
Which of the following is a global biodiversity hotspot in India?
A.Western Ghats
B.Gangetic Plains
C.Thar Desert
D.Deccan Plateau
✅ Answer: A. Western Ghats
💡 Explanation: The Western Ghats are a recognised global hotspot, along with the Himalaya, Indo-Burma and Sundaland.
66
India is considered a:
A.No biodiversity nation
B.Mega-biodiversity nation
C.Desert nation
D.Low biodiversity nation
✅ Answer: B. Mega-biodiversity nation
💡 Explanation: India is one of the 17 mega-diverse countries.
67
Species that are found only in a specific region are called:
A.Extinct species
B.Endangered species
C.Endemic species
D.Invasive species
✅ Answer: C. Endemic species
💡 Explanation: Endemic species are restricted to a particular geographic area.
68
Species whose numbers are so low that they are at risk of extinction are:
A.Endemic species
B.Vulnerable species
C.Abundant species
D.Endangered species
✅ Answer: D. Endangered species
💡 Explanation: Endangered species face a very high risk of extinction in the wild.
69
The major threat to biodiversity is:
A.Habitat loss
B.Reforestation
C.Wildlife sanctuaries
D.Organic farming
✅ Answer: A. Habitat loss
💡 Explanation: Habitat loss and fragmentation are the leading threats to biodiversity.
70
Poaching refers to:
A.Conserving water
B.Illegal hunting of wildlife
C.Breeding animals in zoos
D.Planting trees
✅ Answer: B. Illegal hunting of wildlife
💡 Explanation: Poaching is illegal hunting or capture of wild animals.
71
Biological invasion occurs when:
A.Native species migrate
B.Forests are protected
C.Alien species are introduced and harm native species
D.Animals are kept in zoos
✅ Answer: C. Alien species are introduced and harm native species
💡 Explanation: Introduced species spread, compete with natives and disrupt ecosystems.
72
In-situ conservation means:
A.Conservation in a botanical garden
B.Conservation in a zoo
C.Conservation in a seed bank
D.Conservation in natural habitat
✅ Answer: D. Conservation in natural habitat
💡 Explanation: Species are protected within their natural habitats.
73
Which of the following is an example of In-situ conservation?
A.National Park
B.Aquarium
C.Zoo
D.Botanical Garden
✅ Answer: A. National Park
💡 Explanation: National parks protect species in their natural habitat.
74
Ex-situ conservation means:
A.Hunting
B.Conservation outside natural habitat
C.Conservation in natural habitat
D.Deforestation
✅ Answer: B. Conservation outside natural habitat
💡 Explanation: Species are conserved away from their natural habitat, e.g. in zoos or gene banks.
75
Which of the following is an example of Ex-situ conservation?
A.Wildlife Sanctuary
B.Biosphere Reserve
C.Seed Bank
D.Sacred Groves
✅ Answer: C. Seed Bank
💡 Explanation: Seed banks preserve genetic material outside natural habitats.
76
Man-wildlife conflict often arises due to:
A.Strict conservation laws
B.Increase in natural prey
C.Abundance of forest resources
D.Habitat fragmentation and encroachment
✅ Answer: D. Habitat fragmentation and encroachment
💡 Explanation: Shrinking and fragmented habitats push wildlife into human areas.
77
Ecosystem services include:
A.Water purification and pollination
B.Soil erosion
C.Increased pollution
D.Deforestation
✅ Answer: A. Water purification and pollination
💡 Explanation: Services include water purification, pollination, climate regulation and soil formation.
78
The variety of different ecosystems in a region is known as:
A.Cultural diversity
B.Ecosystem diversity
C.Species diversity
D.Genetic diversity
✅ Answer: B. Ecosystem diversity
💡 Explanation: Ecosystem diversity is the variety of habitats and ecological processes in a region.
79
Which region has the highest biodiversity?
A.Tundra
B.Deserts
C.Tropical rainforests
D.Polar regions
✅ Answer: C. Tropical rainforests
💡 Explanation: Tropical rainforests hold the greatest number of species per unit area.
80
The IUCN Red List keeps a record of:
A.Invasive species
B.Only extinct species
C.Only endemic plants
D.Threatened and endangered species
✅ Answer: D. Threatened and endangered species
💡 Explanation: The Red List assesses the extinction risk of species worldwide.

📌 Climate Change and Greenhouse Gases

81
Which gas is a major contributor to human-caused global warming from fossil-fuel combustion?
A.Nitrogen
B.Carbon dioxide
C.Oxygen
D.Helium
✅ Answer: B. Carbon dioxide
💡 Explanation: Burning coal, oil and natural gas releases CO₂, a long-lived greenhouse gas that contributes to human-caused climate change.

📌 Atmosphere and Ozone Layer

82
The ozone layer that absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation is mainly found in which atmospheric layer?
A.Troposphere
B.Stratosphere
C.Mesosphere
D.Thermosphere
✅ Answer: B. Stratosphere
💡 Explanation: Most atmospheric ozone is concentrated in the stratosphere, where it absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
83
Which group of chemicals has been a major cause of stratospheric ozone depletion?
A.Methane
B.Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
C.Carbon monoxide
D.Nitrogen
✅ Answer: B. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
💡 Explanation: In the stratosphere, chlorine released from CFCs can participate in reactions that destroy ozone.

📌 Air Pollution and Acid Rain

84
Acid rain is mainly associated with atmospheric emissions of which gases?
A.Carbon monoxide and methane
B.Oxygen and hydrogen
C.Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
D.Helium and neon
✅ Answer: C. Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
💡 Explanation: These gases can react in the atmosphere to form sulfuric and nitric acids that return to the surface in wet or dry deposition.

📌 Sustainable Development

85
Sustainable development aims to meet present needs without compromising the ability of whom to meet their own needs?
A.Only present-day industries
B.Future generations
C.Only urban residents
D.Only government agencies
✅ Answer: B. Future generations
💡 Explanation: Sustainable development balances present needs with the capacity of future generations to meet their own needs.

📌 Solid Waste Management

86
In the waste-management hierarchy, which action should generally be prioritized before recycling or disposal?
A.Landfilling
B.Waste prevention or reduction
C.Incineration
D.Disposal in open dumps
✅ Answer: B. Waste prevention or reduction
💡 Explanation: Preventing waste at its source usually conserves more resources than processing waste after it has been created.
87
Composting is most suitable for which type of waste?
A.Glass and metal scrap
B.Electronic circuit boards
C.Biodegradable organic waste
D.Radioactive waste
✅ Answer: C. Biodegradable organic waste
💡 Explanation: Microorganisms decompose suitable food and garden waste into a relatively stable, soil-amending compost.
88
Which greenhouse gas may be generated when organic waste decomposes without oxygen in a landfill?
A.Methane
B.Oxygen
C.Helium
D.Neon
✅ Answer: A. Methane
💡 Explanation: Anaerobic decomposition of organic material in landfills can produce methane, a potent greenhouse gas.
89
What is the main purpose of a sanitary landfill?
A.Open burning of mixed waste
B.Uncontrolled dumping in rivers
C.Controlled disposal of solid waste to minimize contamination
D.Disposal of waste in forests
✅ Answer: C. Controlled disposal of solid waste to minimize contamination
💡 Explanation: Properly designed landfills use engineered barriers and systems to manage leachate and landfill gas.

📌 Air Pollution Control

90
Which action can help reduce transport-related air pollution in cities?
